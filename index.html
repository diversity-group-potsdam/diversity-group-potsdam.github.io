About

We are the Diversity Group Potsdam, part of the Diversity in Education and Development group at the University of Potsdam, led by Prof. Dr. Linda Juang. We're an international team working on issues of culture and migration for youth development and education — studying school belonging, social cohesion in multicultural settings, and the identity development and adjustment of adolescents and young adults with immigrant backgrounds. Every few years we organize and host the Cultural Diversity, Migration, and Education (CDME) Conference, funded by the German Research Foundation (DFG), bringing together researchers and practitioners from education, psychology, sociology, and beyond. Beyond CDME, we regularly present our work at IAIE, IACCP, SRA, DGPS, EADP, IAIR, and GEBF conferences.

Research Areas

School Belonging & Diversity Climate

How diversity climate, norms, and practices in schools relate to students' sense of belonging and adjustment.

Social Cohesion in Multicultural Settings

Intergroup relations, interethnic friendships, and intercultural competence in multiethnic classrooms.

Migration & Education

How migration and cultural diversity contribute to children's educational experiences and outcomes.

Fallstudien zu Diversität & Sozialer Ungerechtigkeit im deutschen Schulkontext

These case studies are the result of a University of Potsdam seminar of the same name, taught by Dr. Miriam Schwarzenthal, Tuğçe Aral, and Sharleen Pevec. The seminar built on Paul Gorski's concept of "Equity Literacy" — teachers' ability to recognize and address social inequities in the classroom along dimensions like socioeconomic background, gender, sexual orientation, disability, migration status, and race. Students interviewed 13 practitioners about their work on diversity and social injustice in schools, then developed pseudonymized written case studies describing real situations, each paired with a step-by-step model for analysis — identifying injustices, considering different perspectives, imagining equitable outcomes, and building short- and long-term solutions — plus discussion questions.

The collection covers four themes: Ethnicity & Racism, Gender & Gender Identity, Disability, and Social Origin.

The case studies are freely available for university lecturers, teachers in professional development, and teacher-training programs to use for reflection. They may not be modified or republished elsewhere without permission from the student authors — for that, contact Dr. Miriam Schwarzenthal at miriam.schwarzenthal@uni-potsdam.de.

News & Events

  • August 2021

    3rd Cultural Diversity, Migration, and Education Conference

    On August 25–27, 2021, we organised and hosted the 3rd CDME Conference — this time completely online, due to the pandemic. We're happy to announce it was a great success, thanks to all the participants, volunteers, presenters, and speakers! The conference had 176 participants from 27 countries, with keynotes from Claude Steele, Naika Foroutan, Sandra Graham, Maurice Crul, and Niobe Way, plus a Pre-Conference Workshop on Open Science, spotlight topics, tea time with keynote speakers, Wonder rooms, and speed mentoring with experts. A warm thank you to everyone who was part of it — see you at the next CDME!

  • December 2020

    Diversity Group Digital Weihnachtsfeier

    Today Sharleen and Miriam organized a wonderful Christmas celebration for us. We played a pub quiz with challenging questions about Christmas — the competition was tight. "Sempatik Santas" (Linda, Hanna, Paul, Özüm) won the pub quiz game 24 to 23 over "die Plätzchen" (Tuğçe, Tendai, David, Karla). We are happy to end a tough year with laughter and joy. Goodbye, 2020!

  • June 2020

    Two Reasons to Celebrate: A Dissertation Prize and a Master's Defense

    We all gathered — physically distant — to celebrate two auspicious occasions after more than two months of separation due to the pandemic. First, Miriam received the University of Potsdam best dissertation prize 2019, as the first female to win this prize! It's a big honor for our team and shows the increasing need and value of addressing immigration and culture issues. Second, Sharleen successfully defended her Master's thesis, "Cultural Identity as Resource: Findings and Implementation in the School Context," and received a 1.2! Congratulations, Sharleen — we're looking forward to reading your future work.

  • March 2019

    Cheers to the 2nd CDME Conference 2018

    Last August we organized and hosted our 2nd Cultural Diversity, Migration, and Education Conference in Potsdam, funded by the German Research Foundation. A warm thank you to all of the participants, presenters, volunteers, and everyone else who was a part of CDME 2018 for making it a successful event! 130 participants from 19 different countries and across disciplines gathered for three days of stimulating presentations, panels, and discussions on how cultural diversity and issues related to migration are potential resources that contribute to children's positive educational experiences and promote school success. A special thanks to Lars Drawert for taking such amazing photos and producing a wonderful video for CDME 2018.

  • February 2018

    Winter Semester Comes to a Close, and With It Our 2017/2018 Lecture Series

    This past Tuesday we hosted our last guest speaker in a semester-long event series organized by the PhD students in our group and funded by the Potsdam Graduate School. We titled the series "Cultural Diversity, Migration, and Education," referencing our 2016 conference of the same name. The series included three engaging talks and one lively panel discussion: Dr. Sabine Glock (Bergische Universität Wuppertal) on teachers' stereotyped expectations, Dr. Daniel Faas (Trinity College Dublin) on EU education policy, a panel on school-community relations in a diverse Germany, and Amena Amer (LSE) on the Prevent Strategy in British schools. We feel honored to have had so many top-notch researchers and practitioners share their time and work with us.

  • July 2017

    IAIR in NYC!

    This past week all of us PhD students plus Maja took part in the 10th Biennial Congress of the International Academy of Intercultural Relations (IAIR), held at the College of Staten Island in New York. Maja won the Early Career Award and co-chaired a symposium on interethnic contact experiences at schools; Sauro, Jana, and Ursula each presented papers from their dissertations. Between sessions we explored "the forgotten borough" — a Staten Island Yankees game, Historic Richmond Town, Fort Wadsworth, and a conference dinner at the Snug Harbor Cultural Center.

  • February 2017

    The Diversity Group Is Looking to Expand!

    We were very happy to hear that Linda and Maja's application for a DFG grant was successful! The grant funds a longitudinal study on the effects of a culture-specific identity affirmation intervention with diverse adolescents in classrooms across Berlin — and to run it, we were looking for another engaged and engaging PhD student to join our research group.

  • January 2017

    Happy New Year from the Diversity in Education and Development Group!

    2016 was a tough year for many of us, but we're looking forward while taking stock of what's behind us. Multiple members of our group had work published — on discrimination and emotion regulation among Latino/a and Asian-heritage college students, on how school cultural diversity climate relates to acculturation and school adjustment in Germany, and on the link between teacher beliefs and practices regarding student diversity. We also joined Campusschulen, a new University of Potsdam program, working with a culturally and socioeconomically diverse secondary school in Berlin, and gave talks from Magdeburg to Humboldt University to the Ministry of Education Baden-Württemberg.

  • December 2016

    Maja Takes Part in a PhD Defense at the University of Leuven

    Maja was invited to be a jury member on the PhD defense of Canan Coskan in the Department of Social and Cultural Psychology at the University of Leuven, Belgium — her first time being "on the other side" of a defense, gown and all. She also gave a talk on one of her recent studies and consulted with eight PhD students in the department about their projects. The visit planted the seed for a joint symposium the two teams would present together at IAIR in New York the following summer.

  • October 2016

    Intercultural Education at the Crossroads

    Ursula and Sauro attended "Mobilities, Transitions, Transformations — Intercultural Education at the Crossroads," the IAIE conference in Budapest, presenting individual papers. Keynotes from Paul Gorski and Audrey Osler offered inspiring suggestions on fostering intercultural education and equity in schools, and a special session chaired by James Banks brought together experts from multiple continents to discuss migration, inclusion, and citizenship education.

  • August 2016

    Back from Japan — IACCP Summer School and Conference

    Three of us spent July 26–30 at an intensive PhD summer school near Nagoya, covering social inequality and the self, culture and cognition, and acculturation and intercultural contact, before the IACCP conference itself drew over 1,000 participants. Our team gave several talks and a poster, and one of us was runner-up for the Harry and Pola Triandis Doctoral Thesis Award. Between sessions there was taiko drumming, a tea ceremony, temple visits, and a teppanyaki dinner in the middle of a monsoon.

  • July 2016

    Survey Data Collection Completed

    We finished collecting survey data from 19 secondary schools and more than 50 classrooms in Berlin — nearly two and a half months of early mornings in principals' offices. The student survey data will support Jana's, Miriam's, and Ursula's dissertations; the teacher survey, Sauro's. The experience was very positive, with real interest from teachers and students in our research topic. Next: working our way through the piles of surveys to get everything entered.

  • July 2016

    Here's to a Successful 1st CDME Conference!

    Our very first Cultural Diversity, Migration, and Education Conference, held July 7–9 at the Neues Palais campus. Thank you to all of the participants, presenters, volunteers, and everyone else who helped make it a resounding success! Three days of keynotes, invited presenters, symposia, and paper sessions, plus an opening reception, a guided park tour, and a conference dinner — with only a brief rain shower along the way. CDME would go on to become a recurring conference, returning in 2018 and 2021.
